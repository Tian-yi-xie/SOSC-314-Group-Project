## Our approach

None of us has a coding background, so this project was also how we learned computational methods. We came up with the research question and the overall design ourselves, then figured out each step of the pipeline from the course readings, package documentation and plenty of trial and error. We used AI much like a tutor or a more experienced classmate. It helped us check that we had understood a method correctly, explained the parts we were stuck on, helped turn our plans into working code, and pointed out where our reasoning was weak. When a suggestion didn't fit our question or our data, we changed it or left it out. The research decisions, the judgment calls and the conclusions are ours.

## How we used AI

**Coding and debugging.** Our scripts build on the code and exercises we learned in class. AI helped us adapt that code to our own data, and to write, fix and review parts of our Python scripts on both platforms. For Tieba, that meant data collection, text cleaning, clustering and the Fightin' Words comparison. For Bilibili, it meant data diagnostics and robustness checks.

**Cluster review and interpretation.** My Thuan doesn't read Chinese, so for the Tieba clusters, AI translated and summarised each cluster's keywords and sample posts and suggested first topic labels. My Thuan used these summaries, along with how similar the clusters were to each other, to decide how they fit together into the final themes (A-I). AI also helped us look up regulatory and platform events, and we only kept events we could confirm in the sources.

**Writing and translation**. AI helped us edit and tighten parts of the report, and gave rough English translations of the Chinese example posts. We reviewed and reworked this material, and a native Chinese speaker on the team checked every translation we used.

**Website.** Tianyi used Claude and Codex to help build, revise and compile the HTML report website and README.
